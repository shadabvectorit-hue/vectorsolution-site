Due diligence — answered upfront

The six questions every overseas client asks

Q1 — Legal entity

Who am I actually contracting with?

VectorIT operates from 5900 Balcones Drive, Suite 100, Austin, Texas 78731, with a US phone line and US-domiciled contracts. You are not wiring money to an anonymous overseas freelancer — you contract with a US-addressable business and pay in USD.

Q2 — Intellectual property

Who owns the code we pay for?

You do — entirely, from the first commit. Work is delivered as work-for-hire into your repositories, your cloud accounts and your domains. We keep no license, no escrow, and no dependency on us. Full IP assignment is written into every agreement.

Q3 — Confidentiality

Can we talk before signing anything?

Yes. We sign your NDA before the first technical conversation, or send ours within the hour if you'd rather use one. Client identities stay confidential by default — one of our largest deployments appears on this site only as an unnamed manufacturing client.

Q4 — Communication

Will we understand each other?

All project communication, documentation, code comments and commit messages are in English. You get a written weekly summary, a working staging URL you can click, and a named engineer who answers you directly — no account manager relaying messages.

Q5 — Data & access

How is our data protected?

Least-privilege access on your systems, credentials held in your password manager rather than ours, and production data never copied to developer machines. Access is revoked the day an engagement ends, and we hand you an offboarding checklist to verify it.

Q6 — Delivery risk

What if it runs late or over budget?

Fixed-scope projects carry a written price and date agreed before work starts; change requests are priced before they're built, never after. You see working software every week, so a slipping project is visible in week two — not at the deadline.
