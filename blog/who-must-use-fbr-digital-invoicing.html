Who has to issue FBR digital invoices — and since when?

Short answer: if your business is sales-tax registered, you are covered — the last phase went live on 31 December 2025. Here is the full schedule, who sits outside it, and what to do if you missed it.

9 August 20266 min readBy Muhammad Shadab

Of everything businesses ask us about FBR digital invoicing, this question comes first: does it apply to us, and since when? The deadline changed four times during 2025, which is why so much conflicting advice is still circulating. Here is where things actually stand as of August 2026.

What digital invoicing is, in one paragraph

The Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) is Pakistan's federal tax authority. Digital invoicing means your billing software reports every sales tax invoice to FBR electronically at the moment you issue it. FBR's system validates the data, assigns a unique FBR invoice number, and sends it back; your software prints that number and a QR code on the invoice your customer receives. Per FBR's own FAQ, it covers every sale you report in Annexure-C of your sales tax return, local and export sales alike. An invoice without an FBR number is, increasingly, not treated as a valid tax invoice at all.

The legal chain, in plain words

The rules arrived as a series of SROs. An SRO (Statutory Regulatory Order) is the notification instrument FBR uses to make and amend rules. Four of them matter here:

  1. SRO 69(I)/2025 (29 January 2025) built the machinery. It rewrote Chapter XIV of the Sales Tax Rules 2006 into one procedure covering electronic invoices, integration of registered persons, and the licensing of integrators.
  2. SRO 709(I)/2025 (22 April 2025) made it universal: all sales-tax-registered persons, corporate and non-corporate, were required to integrate — originally by 1 June 2025 for companies and 1 July 2025 for everyone else.
  3. Those dates proved unworkable and were extended more than once. SRO 1413(I)/2025 (1 August 2025) replaced them with a phased timeline tied to turnover.
  4. SRO 1852(I)/2025 (24 September 2025) set the final schedule, and that one ran to completion.

If an article you found online quotes a different deadline, check its date. It is almost certainly describing one of the superseded notifications above.

The final schedule: five categories, November to December 2025

SRO 1852(I)/2025 split registered persons into five categories, each with three dates: register for digital invoicing on FBR's IRIS portal, finish sandbox testing, then go live. Turnover here means the annual turnover declared in your sales tax returns over the last twelve months.

An AOP is an association of persons: partnerships and similar unincorporated businesses. The last row is the one that closes every gap.

The part to remember: since 31 December 2025, every sales-tax-registered person in Pakistan is required to issue digital invoices — manufacturers, importers, wholesalers, distributors, retailers, corporate or not. That includes ordinary B2B invoices between businesses, not just retail receipts. No blanket extension was notified for 2026.

Who is outside the net

The obligation follows sales tax registration, so as of August 2026 you sit outside it in two cases:

  • You are below the registration threshold. A business not required to hold an STRN (Sales Tax Registration Number) has nothing to integrate. The moment you register, you are in.
  • Your sector is exempt from sales tax and therefore outside the sales tax net altogether.

Two caveats before you relax. First, large Tier-1 retailers were already under FBR's older POS integration regime, which predates digital invoicing and still applies; since SRO 69(I)/2025 both requirements live inside the same chapter of the rules, and many retailers now answer to both. Second, the net is still widening: in February 2026 FBR published a draft amendment to the Income Tax Rules (reported locally as SRO 288(I)/2026) that would pull restaurants, hospitals, private schools, couriers and other service businesses into integration on the income tax side. As of August 2026 it remains a draft, not law — but it shows the direction of travel.

What an invoice must now carry

A compliant digital invoice shows, at minimum:

  • The FBR invoice number — built from your NTN (National Tax Number) or CNIC, the letters DI, and a 13-digit timestamp. The official API documentation's example is 7000007DI1747119701593: 22 characters with a 7-digit NTN. Blogs describing a "26-digit invoice number" are quoting a format that does not match the current spec.
  • A QR code — Version 2, 25 by 25 modules, printed at 1 inch by 1 inch, per FBR's official API documentation (DI API v1.12). The 7x7 mm figure you may see elsewhere comes from the older POS receipt specification, not the digital invoicing spec.
  • The FBR Digital Invoicing logo alongside the QR code.
  • Correct product data behind the scenes. Every line item needs an HS code (Harmonized System code, the commodity classification FBR validates against) and a unit of measurement that matches FBR's list for that HS code. In our experience, mismatched HS codes and units are the most common reason invoices bounce.

Not integrated yet? Where you actually stand

Every deadline above has passed, and 2026 is the enforcement year. The first-default penalty for failing to integrate is Rs 500,000 under the Sales Tax Act, and it escalates from there; we walk through the full ladder, premises sealing, and the new Finance Act 2026 de-registration and blacklisting powers in the next article.

Three things worth knowing before you panic:

  • The Commissioner can extend a taxpayer's integration deadline by up to 60 days in aggregate, granted in 15-day intervals, with paper invoices permitted during the extension. Breathing room exists, but you have to ask for it.
  • The connection itself runs through PRAL (Pakistan Revenue Automation Ltd), FBR's own licensed integrator, which provides integration services free of charge by law. What PRAL does not provide is the work on your side of the pipe: your software still has to speak FBR's language.
  • Section 64D of the Income Tax Ordinance, added by Finance Act 2026, gives a 10% tax credit on what you spend to integrate, claimable in the year of installation.
